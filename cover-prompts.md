# Cover prompts

Generated header images for the blog. All 28 prompts use one recipe: same
palette, same stylisation clause, same negatives, `21:9`. Only the scene
sentence and the one-line absurdist beat change between them. Covers land in a
post bundle as `cover.png` — see the Cover art section of `README.md`.

Palette is the site's own tokens, which now run the other way round: the ten
covers in `covers/` came first and the CSS tokens were sampled from them.
Paper `#FAF6EA`, water blue `#A9CFD2`, peach `#E4C0A0`, sage `#C3D3C3`, sand
`#DCCFAE`, olive stone `#C2C6B2`, deep teal `#26706C` for accents, slate-green
`#1F2E2B` for outlines. No red anywhere. Adding more colours than this reads as
noise; lilac `#D6D0E4` and dusty pink `#EDD2CC` were both tried and cut.

Two clauses are doing more work than they look:

- **Full-bleed, first sentence.** `packaging art style` makes models draw the
  box — cream margin, sometimes a caption bar. The edge-to-edge instruction plus
  `borders, frames, margins, white edges, poster frames, caption bars` in the
  negatives is what suppresses it. Don't drop either half.
- **The hedges on stylisation.** `very gently exaggerated - restrained, never
  cartoonish` is the whole dial. Remove them and it swings to full cartoon in
  one step. If a result is too stylised, cut the proportions clause first.

For Midjourney rather than Gemini, prose negatives are ignored: move everything
after the final `No` into `--no ...` and append `--ar 21:9`. The README's cover
art section has the MJ-specific notes.


## 1. Workshop bench, three computers  *(the reference image)*

```
Pastel risograph illustration, full-bleed, filling the entire canvas edge to edge with no border or margin. Cramped 1990s workshop bench seen straight on: three beige computers in a row - first with case open and boards exposed, second dark and unplugged, third on with a soft sage screen - tangled cables looping between them, toolbox, screwdriver, pliers, stacked disk boxes, coffee mug, small CRT monitor on shelf above, power strip trailing off right edge. A single houseplant grows out of the open computer's empty drive bay, watered by a tiny watering can left on the bench beside it - played completely straight, as if that is where plants go. Objects spread full width, layered and overlapping, busy but calm. 1990s Japanese software packaging art style. Slightly stylised: each object simplified towards its essential silhouette, mechanical detail reduced to a few confident marks, larger flat areas of colour, uniform thin outlines, proportions very gently exaggerated - restrained, never cartoonish. Palette: warm cream paper #FAF6EA, pale water blue #A9CFD2, soft peach #E4C0A0, sage green #C3D3C3, warm sand #DCCFAE, olive stone #C2C6B2, deep teal #26706C accents, dark slate-green #1F2E2B outlines only. Assign the colours to individual objects by name rather than blending them; teal used sparingly, on one or two objects only. Flat two tone shading, coarse dithering, halftone grain, low res pixel texture, slight misregistration between colour layers. Soft even light, low contrast. Wide 21:9 landscape. No borders, frames, margins, white edges, poster frames, caption bars, credit lines, signatures, text, numbers, logos, people, photorealism, 3D, gloss, neon, glow, or heavy shadows.
```
